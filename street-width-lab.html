How the experiment works

You click wheel hubs and street-edge points in several Mapillary frames. Each car's front and rear hub on the same side are a scale bar of known length (the wheelbase). The exported JSON goes to a bundle adjustment that solves image orientations, the points and one scale factor for Mapillary's trajectory, then reports the widths with their standard deviations.

Workflow

  1. Paste your Mapillary client token (create one at mapillary.com → Dashboard → Developers). It stays in this browser.
  2. Draw the segment along the street axis, set the buffer and search. Keep one sequence to start.
  3. Add a car, type the wheelbase, select Front hub and click it in every frame where you see it well. Then the rear hub.
  4. Add edge points in groups: curb_L, curb_R, facade_L, facade_R. Click each in two or more frames.
  5. Type your tape measurements under Widths and tape measurements, then export the JSON.

What helps the adjustment

  • Click every hub in every frame where it is visible within about 12 m. In simulation this halved the width error compared with clicking 4 frames.
  • 360° sequences see parked cars from the side and give several times better scale. Turn on Full resolution when clicking panoramas.
  • After a point has two clicks, a dashed ring shows where it should be in the other frames. With one click you get the epipolar curve. A click far from the ring means a wrong match.
  • Hub centre means the centre of the wheel cap, not the centre of the tyre ellipse.

Conventions

Poses are Mapillary's computed_rotation (angle-axis, ENU to camera, x right, y down, z forward) and computed_geometry/computed_altitude. The pose check in the quick look compares the camera axis with computed_compass_angle; it should be within a degree or two.
